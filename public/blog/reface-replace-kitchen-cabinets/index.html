Should You Reface or Replace Kitchen Cabinets in Calgary?

By Alberta Elite Construction - Home Remodeling Calgary Editorial Team  ·  3 min read

If your cabinet boxes are solid and you only want a new look, refacing or refinishing is usually the smarter, lower-cost choice. Replace when boxes are rotten, warped, infested, or when you need a different layout or built-in appliance panels.

This article helps you decide by defining the two options, listing clear red flags for replacement, showing Calgary-specific cost bands and timeline differences, and giving a step-by-step decision checklist so you can prepare for an on-site estimate.

TL;DR

  • Refacing or refinishing saves money and time when cabinet boxes are structurally sound and you do not need a layout change.
  • Replace cabinets when you see water damage, rot, collapsed boxes, or when you want new built-in appliances or a different footprint.
  • Plan for refacing as a short project measured in days; full replacement generally requires weeks and coordination with trades.
  • Local factors in Calgary such as seasonal humidity, condo rules, and material lead times should influence scheduling.
  • Take photos, measure linear feet, and get an on-site assessment to confirm hidden issues before committing.

What we mean by reface, refinish and replace

Refinish: sanding and repainting or staining existing doors and exposed surfaces. It is mainly a surface treatment and works best on solid, well-made doors.

Reface: replacing door and drawer fronts and applying a new veneer or laminate to the visible cabinet boxes. The internal boxes remain in place, which saves time and waste.

Replace: removing existing cabinets and installing new boxes and fronts. This is the option you choose when you need layout changes, different depths or full-height cabinetry. Replacement can be stock, semi-custom, or fully custom depending on budget and goals.

When you should replace your cabinets

Replace when any of these apply:

  • ✓Visible rot, mould or persistent odour that indicates water intrusion
  • ✓Boxes are warped, collapsed or missing structural fasteners
  • ✓You want to move plumbing, add an island, or integrate appliances
  • ✓You need a higher quality or longer-lasting cabinet construction
Watch for this: If you find mould or structural rot, pause cosmetic upgrades and book a professional inspection. Covering damage with paint or veneer will not resolve health or stability risks.

Local insight

Calgary-specific considerations

Calgary winters bring large seasonal temperature and humidity swings. Wood cabinetry can expand and contract more noticeably than in milder climates, so check for long-term gaps or sticking doors after a seasonal change. If you live in a condominium, building rules may restrict hours for noisy demo or require pre-approval for layout changes. Lead times for specialty doors, custom finishes and high-demand hardware can increase in late spring and early fall; allow extra weeks if you need a specific colour or custom panels. A local general contractor will handle permit coordination and advise whether your project crosses any building code triggers.

Frequently asked questions

Will refacing hide water damage?

No. Refacing addresses surface appearance. If there is water damage or mould in the cabinet boxes, those problems must be repaired or replaced first. A professional inspection will find hidden damage under toe kicks, behind appliances and under sinks.

How long does a typical refinish last in Calgary?

A good refinish can last 5 to 15 years depending on materials, traffic and whether the finish is properly maintained. Seasonal humidity in Calgary can affect wood components, so choose moisture-stable finishes and monitor door fit after seasonal changes.

Do I need a permit to change my kitchen layout in Calgary?

Minor cosmetic changes usually do not require a building permit. However, moving plumbing, changing load-bearing walls, or altering electrical work often triggers permit requirements. Check with your contractor or the City of Calgary building services for specifics on your scope.

Can you replace just a few cabinets instead of the whole kitchen?

Yes. Targeted replacement of problem areas or the introduction of new tall units or an island is common. Keep in mind that matching finishes and door styles can be harder with partial replacement, so plan for samples and mock-ups.

What should I expect at an on-site estimate?

Expect a contractor to inspect cabinet boxes, measure, list any hidden issues, review finish and hardware options, and provide a written quote that separates materials, labour, disposal and any required trades. Bring photos and measurements to help the estimator give a more accurate price.
